Tools

The deck is generated from the primary sources by the programs in tools/. The sources are in src/ (fetched by tools/fetch.sh); everything else can be regenerated from them by tools/make.sh. The decks I.1.deck ... IV.deck and contents.deck and layout.conf are generated: do not edit them, edit the hand-checked data (overrides.py, observed.txt) and regenerate.

The pipeline

fetch.sh                      sources -> src/
djvu_lines.py                 OCR (djvu.xml) -> lines and word boxes
witnesses.py, scans.py        transcriptions, scans -> pages
collate.py (+ overrides.py)   witnesses -> established text (collation.json)
imgcmp.py                     word images of 1939 and 1958 -> similarity
candidates.py                 -> candidate variants
reocr.py                      candidates -> second OCR reading
revsheet.py                   candidates -> contact sheets, checked by eye
observed.txt (+ variants.py)  the variants found
finwake.py                    finwake.com chapters -> glosses located
audio.py                      captions of readings -> where they start
rose.py                       variants of 2010 (jjda.ie) -> placed
notons.py                     notebook links (jjda.ie) -> placed
mkdeck.py                     text + variants + glosses -> decks
make.sh                       collate.py ... rose.py, mkdeck.py

The programs

fetch.sh: downloads the witnesses into src/: the two Oxford Text Archive transcriptions (from the Wayback Machine, because the archive is behind a bot challenge), the scans of 1939 and 1958 and their OCR from the Internet Archive.

djvu_lines.py: extracts the lines of the OCR of a scan (an archive.org djvu.xml file), with the bounding boxes of the lines and of the words.

witnesses.py: reads the transcriptions W and T into a common form: for every page, the lines of the main text and, in II.2, of the left and right marginalia and the footnotes. Decodes the escapes of W (accented letters, dashes) and the code page of T.

scans.py: maps the scans to printed pages (by the position in the 1939 scan, by shared word trigrams in the 1958 scan), drops page numbers and printers' marks, and, in II.2, separates the main text, the marginalia and the footnotes by their position on the page.

fweet_rules.py: the facts of Fweet's line numbering: which pages have 36 (or 37) lines, which have marginalia and footnotes, which verses are printed with a hanging indent. Used to check the result.

collate.py: establishes the text. For every page and component, the physical lines come from a scan whose count of lines is right, the words from W (or T where W lacks a line). The tokens of W are aligned with those of T and of both scans; disagreements of W and T are decided by the scans. Every decision is logged. Also records the layout: centred lines, the position of the marginalia, the continuation lines of verses, the lines set to the full measure (which are justified) and the measure of each page relative to the regular pages.

overrides.py: corrections of the collation decided by looking at the page images: substitutions, replaced components (some marginalia of II.2), inserted lines (the figures and the musical score), centred lines.

imgcmp.py: compares the image of every word in the 1939 scan with its image in the 1958 scan (binarised, trimmed, scaled and correlated).

candidates.py: the words whose two images or two OCR readings differ in a way that OCR confusions do not explain.

reocr.py: reads the candidate words again in both scans with one OCR engine (RapidOCR), so that identical print is read identically. Also used for the words where both scans agree against the text. Runs in a separate Python environment (~/.venvs/fw, with rapidocr_onnxruntime and yt-dlp, which fetch.sh uses for the captions of the readings).

revsheet.py: contact sheets of the remaining candidates, each line from 1939 above the same line from 1958, for checking by eye.

sheet.py, crop.py: crops of lines of the scans, used to decide the disagreements of the transcriptions.

finwake.py: locates the glosses of finwake.com in the text: the words of its chapters (src/finwake/), where the glossed words are links, are aligned with the words of the established text; each glossed word gives a note linking to its gloss (finwake.json). Links to anchors missing from the gloss pages (src/finwake/glosses/) are repaired.

rose.py: the variants of the edition of 2010: parses the pages "1939 -> 2010" of the James Joyce Digital Archive (src/rose/cmp/), with the changes of 2010 and the page breaks of both editions, aligns them with the text of each page here and places every change on its line (rose.json, also with the pages of 2010 that each page here spans).

notons.py: locates the "notons" of the James Joyce Digital Archive: the phrases of its text of 1939 (src/rose/l39/) linked to notebook entries are aligned with the text here; each gives a note linking to the phrase on the archive, labelled with its entry (the labels of the notebooks are in src/rose/notebooks.txt) (notons.json).

audio.py: locates the beginnings of the recorded readings listed in src/audio/readings.txt: the automatic captions of each recording are matched against the text of its chapter; the line where they start gets a note linking to the recording at that time (audio.json).

ocrnoise.py: a table of the usual OCR confusions (rn/m, h/b, l/1, ...).

observed.txt, variants.py: the differences between 1939 and 1958 seen in the images, and their conversion to apparatus notes.

mkdeck.py: writes the decks: a card per page in the series of the book, of its book and of its chapter; a fragment per line, identified by its line number; marginalia and footnotes of II.2; apparatus notes; links to the page images; the contents card. The decks contain only the text, the identifiers and the notes; the layout of the lines is in a generated style sheet, layout.conf, which selects a line by its anchor (a[name="p004-17"] + p). Lines are justified, except those not set to the full measure in print; where the printed measure is narrower than usual (II.2, footnotes), its width is computed from the widths of the lines in the text face (src/fonts).

make.sh: runs collate.py, finwake.py, audio.py, rose.py, notons.py and mkdeck.py.
