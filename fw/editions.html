Editions

The text of this deck is established from four witnesses: two scanned printings and two independent transcriptions. The apparatus records the differences between three editions: the two scanned printings, the first edition of 1939 and the corrected printing of 1958, and the edition of 2010 by Danis Rose and John O'Hanlon, which revises the text throughout.

The editions

1939. James Joyce, Finnegans Wake. London: Faber and Faber; New York: The Viking Press, 4 May 1939. Set in Monotype Fournier and printed by R. MacLehose and Company, The University Press, Glasgow. The first edition: the British and American issues were printed from the same setting. The copy used is the scan of the Faber issue in the Digital Library of India (from the Rashtrapati Bhavan Library).

1958. James Joyce, Finnegans Wake. New York: The Viking Press, 1958 and later printings. Photo-offset from the plates of the first edition, with Joyce's "Corrections of Misprints" (printed separately in 1945 and bound into copies from 1947) entered into the text. Some corrections required resetting a passage, so that a few lines are divided differently from 1939. The copy used is the scan from the University of Toronto (Fisher Library).

2010. James Joyce, Finnegans Wake, edited by Danis Rose and John O'Hanlon. Mousehole: Houyhnhnm Press, 2010; reprinted as The Restored Finnegans Wake, London: Penguin Classics, 2012. Some 9 000 changes to the text of 1939, of words, punctuation and spacing, and passages restored, from the manuscripts, typescripts and proofs. Set anew, in 493 pages: its pages and lines are not those of 1939. Not available as images; its text, with every change marked, is on the James Joyce Digital Archive.

The page and line division of 1939 and 1958 is the same (except where noted in the apparatus), and is that of all the later Faber and Viking printings and of the line numbers used by Fweet. The pages and lines here are those of 1939 and 1958; the edition of 2010 enters only through the apparatus, and at the foot of each page, through a link to the pages of 2010 that it spans.

The transcriptions

W. The transcription by David Wilson of the Faber edition (1964), preserving the line and page breaks. Oxford Text Archive 1709 (1992; archived copy).

T. The transcription by Donald F. Theall, Joan Benedict Theall and Ray Fritz-Németh of the Faber second edition (1950), with the published errata incorporated; the "Trent text", the source of most electronic texts of the book. Oxford Text Archive 1696 (1992; archived copy).

Both are distributed under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 licence. Each has its own errors (Fweet lists more than three hundred in T): lost and doubled lines, dropped words, misreadings.

The established text

The text is that of the corrected Faber and Viking printings. Where W and T agree, their reading is taken; where they disagree, the reading of the printings, as read from the scans, decides. A little under three hundred places were decided by looking at the page images. See Tools.

Joyce's sigla, the signs for the characters of the book (E for Humphrey Chimpden Earwicker, A for Anna Livia Plurabelle and so on), are set in the Fweet Figla font of Raphael Slepon, after a font of Michael Powers, with thanks. In the deck files a siglum is written as Fweet writes it, the letter between asterisks. The few sigla that the font lacks (the turned and reversed forms on 18.36, 36.17, 121.03, 121.07 and 266.22) are written with the closest Unicode characters. The musical scores and the figures are images cropped from the 1939 scan.

The notes

The right margin carries the notes "ט word" (tet), which link the word to its gloss on finwake.com; they are made by aligning the text of finwake.com, where the glossed words are links, with the text of this deck (about 51 000 glosses, some on parts of words; about 500 could not be placed). Only the links are here, not the glosses. Where finwake.com links a gloss to an anchor that its page does not have, the link goes to the anchor that was meant; about 2 400 glosses that are not on the free pages of finwake.com are left out.

The notes "Ω phrase: entry" (omega) link a phrase to the entry of the notebook (or notesheet, or copybook) in which Joyce noted it, as transcribed on the James Joyce Digital Archive, where these links are called "notons". The entry is named as on the archive: the notebook, its page and the item on the page ("N18 (VI.B.8): 185(i)"). The archive links about 24 000 phrases of the text to their entries; about 23 600 are given here. The link goes to the phrase in the archive's text of 1939: clicking the phrase there shows the entry in a window, with its source, its use in the drafts and links to them. This works for every notebook, although most full transcriptions of the notebooks are not public on the archive (the Joyce estate did not permit their publication). Only the links are taken from the archive: no part of its transcriptions is copied here.

Two kinds of notes are footnotes, at the foot of the page, each starting with the number of its line, and underlined in the same colour as the words of the line it refers to: the notes of the apparatus (below), which start with ב, and the notes "ק" (qof, for "qol", voice), which mark the lines where recorded readings start and link to the recording at that point; the places are found by matching the automatic captions of the recordings against the text.

The apparatus

Where 1939, 1958 and the edition of 2010 differ, a footnote on the line gives the variant, in the usual form of a critical apparatus, at the foot of the page as in a printed edition. To tell them from other notes, they start with the letter beth, ב (for "variorum"):

ב lemma] SIGLUM; reading SIGLUM

The lemma is the reading of the established text, followed by the sigla of the editions that have it; after the semicolon are the other readings, each followed by the sigla of the editions that have it. "om." means that the printing omits the lemma. For example, on 4.14 the established text reads "was", as does 1958, while 1939 reads "waz":

ב was] 1958; waz 1939

and on 3.02 the established text, with 1939 and 1958, reads "commodius", where 2010 reads "commodious":

ב commodius] 1939 1958; commodious 2010

A passage that the two printings divide into lines differently is marked on its first line. Differences in spacing, broken or battered type and other accidents of printing that are not readings are not recorded.

The differences were found by comparing the images of every word of the two scans (as located by their OCR) and reading again, with a second OCR engine, every word whose two images or two OCR readings differed; the remaining candidates were then checked by eye. Differences that both OCR engines read identically in both scans (for example, a changed punctuation mark that the engines did not notice) can have been missed.

At the bottom of each page are links to the images of that page in both printings, and to the corresponding pages of the edition of 2010.

The variants of 2010

The variants of 2010 are those recorded by its editors on the James Joyce Digital Archive, which shows, for every section of the book, the text of 1939 with every change of 2010 marked (the pages "1939 → 2010"); about 7 900 notes of the apparatus record them. Each change is placed on its line here by aligning that text with the text of this deck. Where the established text already agrees with 2010 (most often in Joyce's own corrections of 1939, which 2010 also adopts), there is no note of 2010, but the siglum 2010 is added to the reading it shares in the note of 1939 and 1958. A reading of 2010 longer than a dozen words is abbreviated, and linked to its place on the Archive. About 650 changes (some in passages that 2010 moves, and in the marginalia and footnotes of II.2) could not be placed. The edition of 2010 is in copyright: its readings are cited here, as in any apparatus.

The text of 1939 on the Archive is not always that of the printing (it corrects some misprints, and has a few errors of its own): it serves only to place the changes. The readings of 1939 and 1958 in the notes are those of the scans.

The foot of each page links to the pages of 2010 that correspond to it, on the Archive, at the words with which the page begins (the link is a text fragment: browsers that do not support them open the section at its top).
